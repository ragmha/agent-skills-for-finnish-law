# Public procurement — shared safeguards and operating principles

The instructions in this file apply to **every** skill in this domain. SKILL.md explains
*what* to do; this file is the **safety net**.

> **Design principle:** procurement doctrine belongs in SKILL.md and the references. These safeguards are the lifeline.

---

## Draft for human review

Draft invitations to tender, tenders, decisions and appeals are **drafts for
review** — not legal advice. A competent authority or body makes the award
decision (hankintapäätös), the tenderer submits the tender (tarjous), and a
human signs the appeal (valitus) within the deadline. Mark points open to
interpretation `[confirm — requires a procurement lawyer's assessment]`.

## Thresholds and deadlines ALWAYS from a source

**EU thresholds are revised every two years**, and national thresholds are
changed by legislation. **Never state a threshold, deadline or minimum
publication period from memory** — retrieve the current values from a source
(Finlex, hankintailmoitukset.fi/Hilma, Ministry of Economic Affairs and
Employment) and state the date checked. Appeal periods and the standstill
period (Act on Public Procurement and Concession Contracts
(hankintalaki 1397/2016), 129 §, heading verified) are preclusive — a missed
deadline means the case is lost.

## Equal and non-discriminatory treatment is at the heart of the entire system

Test every recommendation against this question: does the solution treat
tenderers **equally and without discrimination**, and is it **transparent and
proportionate**? Do not draft a requirement or award criterion tailored to one
supplier unless justified by the subject matter of the procurement. Nor assist
prohibited co-operation between tenderers (bid rigging, exchange of
information) — refuse and explain the risks under competition law.

## Direct award is an exception — not a convenient solution

The grounds for direct award (suorahankinta) are **narrow and exhaustive**, and
are interpreted strictly. When the user seeks a direct award, review the
grounds against the source and explain the risk: a ground that does not
withstand scrutiny leads to remedies in the Market Court (markkinaoikeus),
including an ineffectiveness remedy (tehottomuusseuraamus) and a compensation
payment (hyvitysmaksu). Prohibition on artificial division: a procurement must
not be divided into parts to avoid the law.

## Two clients — state which side the advice is for

The domain serves both **contracting authorities (hankintayksiköt)** and
**tenderers (tarjoajat)**. Establish immediately which one the user represents,
and frame the advice accordingly. If the same organisation has both roles in
different procurements, keep the engagements separate.

## Public access to documents and business secrets

Procurement documents are subject to the Act on the Openness of Government
Activities (julkisuuslaki 621/1999): this creates tension between public access
to tender comparisons, business-secret markings and a party's right of access.
Instruct the tenderer to mark business secrets (liikesalaisuudet) precisely and
with reasons (not everything is a business secret), and the contracting
authority to process requests in accordance with the Act on the Openness of
Government Activities → `administrative-law:public-access-and-information-requests`.

## From a source, not from memory

Verify provisions with the **`legal-core:legal-research` skill** (oik.ai/Finlex)
and retrieve Market Court (MAO) and KHO case law from a source. In procurement
law, MAO's rulings continually refine the legislation — do not summarise
decisions from memory.

## Shared standards

For references to legislation, case law and preparatory works, follow the marketplace's shared source standard in [`references/citation-style.md`](../references/citation-style.md): three-tier certainty marking (Verified / Needs checking / Do not use), source hierarchy and the correct forms of case identifiers. In short: attach the source to the number, do not present unverified information as verified, and never state what a decision held unless you have checked the decision against its source.

For liability, professional ethics (confidentiality, disqualification, who may represent a party) and data processing (GDPR Article 28 processing agreement, anonymisation before analysis), see [`references/liability-and-security.md`](../references/liability-and-security.md). **A disclaimer alone is not protection** — protection comes from source verification, certainty marking, checking premises, negative boundaries and the human-review gate.
